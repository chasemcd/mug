Between the two

The first pass was generic. The second took the containers away and went too far: a page of ruled text, where an action was a word with a line under it and a participant had to read the screen to find what was clickable. This one puts the containers back and gives them a reason to be there.

The conversation Bubbles on opposite sides, the wait for a reply, a reply that does not come. The judgement Two replies were written, and the one you pick is the one you get. The comparison Two rounds that were played, and two things that were written. The shared room Several people, channels, and everybody with a name and a mark.

What it takes from each

  • From the first pass The bubble, the card, the filled button, and the two sides of a conversation. A participant should never have to read a screen to find what can be pressed.
  • From the second pass Charter for everything somebody said or wrote, a plain grotesk for everything the screen is asking, one accent used for actions and nothing else, and no shadow anywhere. A surface is held by an edge.
  • From neither The hover fill. A pointer darkens an edge that is already drawn, or draws a rule under a cell. It never puts a new fill on a shape that had none, which is the cheapest signal there is.

What is kept from the earlier findings

  • Axes are cells Not a slider. A slider has to start somewhere, and a participant who never touches it still sends the value it started on, so the study records a judgement nobody gave. Nothing is chosen at the start.
  • The pair stretches Both columns are one grid with one track rule, and both actions sit on one line. An affordance must not favour a reply for being short.
  • Names, not "Them" The wire already carries author_actor_id on every message. In a room of four, the client today collapses all of them to "Them".
