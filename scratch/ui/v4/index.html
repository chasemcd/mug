The whole set

Every screen a participant reaches, in one design language, with the parts that carry a judgement given a stronger hand. Nothing here is wired into the platform.

Talk The conversation Bubbles on opposite sides, the wait for a reply, a reply that does not come. Judge The judgement Read, compare, choose — numbered, in that order, down the panel. Judge The comparison Two rounds you can watch again, and two things that were written. Talk The shared room Channels, and everybody in the room with a name and a mark. Play A game and a conversation Two panes, a status line, and a plain answer to "where are my keys going?". Read Reading, answering, finishing Instructions, a form, and the last screen with the code.

What carries the identity

  • The notch Every surface that holds something somebody said or made has one square corner where the others are round. A bubble, a card, a badge, and the mark in the head are the same shape at four sizes.
  • Two faces Charter for anything a person said, wrote, or is asked. A plain grotesk for anything the screen says about itself. A participant always knows which is which.
  • One blue, one amber Blue is what you can do and what you chose. Amber is what is live now: the clock, the score, a reply that failed. Nothing else takes a colour, and neither side of a comparison ever takes one.

What makes it intuitive

  • Numbered work A judgement says 1 read, 2 compare, 3 choose, in that order, down the panel. Nobody has to work out what to do first.
  • It says back what you said Every scale writes the answer under it in words — "You said: A is more helpful". A mark on a row of cells is never the only record of what somebody meant, and a misread scale is caught by the person who answered it.
  • The badge anchors the scale The two ends of a comparison carry the same badges as the two options, so which end is which is read and not remembered.
  • It counts what is left "2 of 4 answered" on the judgement, and on the form.
  • Nothing is preset No scale starts in the middle, so the study never records a judgement nobody gave. The tie is a place somebody chooses, and it carries no tint that would recommend it.

What is still refused

  • The hover fill A pointer darkens an edge that is already drawn, or draws a rule under a cell. It never puts a fill on a shape that had none.
  • The shadow Nothing floats. A surface is held by an edge.
  • A side that wins The two options are one grid with one track rule, they stretch together, both badges are the same ink, and the chosen fill is drawn only after a choice.
