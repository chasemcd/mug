The participant interface

Six mock-ups of one design. Nothing here is wired into the platform: they are static files, and they are made to be argued with.

This is the first pass, and it is the generic one -- rounded panels, soft shadows, one teal accent, and a rectangle that lights up under the pointer. The second pass gives two directions that are none of those things →

The three rules this design keeps

  1. The screen never says whether the other party is a person or a model. Only the study knows that, and only the study may say it. So there is no robot icon, no "Assistant", and no brand mark on a reply.
  2. Two things a participant is asked to compare must look the same. The two cards are one grid with one track rule, and no rule anywhere styles "the first one". A card that is wider or brighter is a measurement error, not a style choice.
  3. Every control keeps a visible focus ring. A game pane and a chat pane are changed with the Tab key, so a participant who can not see the focus can not play.

What is different from the interface today

Today Here
A message is <p><strong>You: </strong>text</p>. A turn is a row: a mark, a name, and the words, with air between turns.
The message box is an <input> at 70% width. A box that grows to what is written and stops, with the send inside it.
Everybody who is not you is "Them", in a room of four as well. Every other party gets a name and a colour. The server already sends author_actor_id on every message; the screen throws it away.
A comparison is a column of default buttons. Two cards of one width, and for a round that was played, the round.
A scale is seven loose radio dots with no words at the ends. One row of marks, with both ends written out.
An axis is a slider that starts in the middle. It draws a filled bar to the left before anybody touches it, and a participant who never touches it still sends the middle value. A row of marks with nothing chosen. "The same" is a mark somebody picks, not a place the control was parked.
The pane that holds the keys is a blue outline and nothing else. An outline and a badge that says where the keys are going.
Dark only, #0f1115, at every screen size. Light and dark, from one set of tokens.

What to argue with

  • Flat or bubbles. Mock-up 1 has both. Flat reads as a document and holds long replies better; bubbles make it obvious which words are yours. Pick one.
  • Light or dark as the default. A participant is on an unknown screen in an unknown room, and a study is often read before it is played.
  • The two cards stack on a narrow screen. Side by side, the order is shuffled and carries no signal. Stacked, one is above the other and is read first. That is a question about the data, not about the layout.
  • Colour names for the other parties. They say nothing about whether a party is a person or a model, which is the rule. Somebody may still find them odd.
  • The step marks. They say where the participant is and promise no time, because no study can honestly give one. A study with 30 steps needs a different mark.
